Why Is My iPhone Battery Draining So Fast?

A day of fast drain is usually a temporary, explainable situation — not a signal that your phone is dying.

You're probably here because the battery percentage is dropping faster than it used to — maybe today, maybe for a few days — and you're wondering whether something is wrong. It's natural to jump to the worst case: the battery is worn out, or the phone needs replacing. Before any of that, let's look at what the phone itself says. That's a calmer question, and it usually has a clear answer.
Direct answer: a single fast-draining day does not mean your battery is failing. The useful explanations to check are temporary and normal: a recent iOS update is still finishing background work, you used the phone more heavily than usual, you spent time somewhere with weak signal, or one app was working hard in the background. The first step is to open Settings > Battery and read what actually used the power — not to start turning settings off by feel. If the drain keeps happening without an obvious change in how you use the phone, check the Battery information for a pattern rather than treating one bad day as proof that something is wrong.

First, figure out whether this is actually unusual

Not every fast day is the same problem. Sort yourself into one of these first:

  • It started today, out of nowhere. Check the Battery page (below) before changing anything. A single day is usually explainable — heavy use, a long call, weak signal, or an app you left running.
  • It started right after an iOS update. Apple says update-related tasks can keep running in the background and temporarily affect battery life and heat. This usually settles after a few days — that's expected, not damage.
  • It's been several days in a row. Now it's worth a real look: what does Battery show, and did your actual usage change? That's the difference between "a heavy day" and "a pattern."
  • It's a brand-new iPhone. New phones spend their first days indexing, syncing, and setting up — which is genuinely hungry. If that's your situation, the guide about fast drain on a new iPhone is the more relevant one.

There is no reliable "your battery should drop X percent per hour" number, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. Your phone's own battery chart is the honest reference — use it instead.

Check Battery before changing settings

Settings > Battery is the evidence room. It tells you what used the power, and it's far more useful than turning things off by feel:

  • Daily Usage (on iOS 26 and later) compares today's usage with the past seven days, and tells you whether today is higher, lower, or about normal. That single comparison answers "is this actually unusual?" faster than any guessing.
  • App and System Activity Usage lists the apps and system activity that used battery today, with the biggest users at the top. Tap any app to see its own history and what kind of activity it was.
  • Activity labels name the cause directly — for example Background Activity (music, location tracking, or syncing running in the background), Notifications (an app waking the screen), or No Mobile Coverage and Low Signal (the phone working harder to stay connected). Each label points to a specific, small fix.
  • Insights at the top of the Battery screen tell you when background system work is happening. On iOS 18 and later you may see Ongoing iOS Update or Ongoing Device Setup — the phone is still finishing work in the background. That work temporarily affects battery life and temperature, and things return to normal when it finishes.

The emotional payoff here is real: if Battery shows a clear reason — an update still settling, an app with hours of background activity, a "low signal" label — then you have a plausible explanation for the drain — it doesn't automatically mean the phone is failing.

When an update makes battery life feel worse

If you recently updated iOS and battery life seems worse, Apple's guidance is to wait a few days and check again. Right after an update the phone may be re-indexing, refreshing system assets, and finishing setup in the background — you can use it normally, but those tasks draw power. You'll often see the Ongoing iOS Update insight in Settings > Battery while that's happening.

What that means for you: give it a few days of normal use before judging. And don't respond to a post-update week by restarting, erasing, or restoring the phone — that only creates more background work and makes the comparison impossible.

When the battery is actually worth checking

Most fast-drain days never reach this point. You move from "today felt off" to "worth checking further" only when:

  • The drain is persistent — it keeps happening day after day, not one bad day.
  • Your usage hasn't obviously changed, and Battery shows no clear cause like an update or a specific app.
  • One app is consistently using far more than your actual use of it explains.
  • Battery Health shows a real signal. Check it in Settings > Battery > Battery Health (iPhone 15 and later) or Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging (iPhone 14 and earlier). If it shows Service, Apple recommends having the battery looked at — that's the official cue to consider service, and it's a much clearer signal than any percentage you've heard about online.

Notice what's not on that list: there is no official "below 80% means replace it" rule for drain, and a worn battery is only one of several possible causes. Let the phone's own indicators point, rather than a number someone quoted you.

You probably don't need to

  • Buy a new phone because of one fast day. A single day of drain is not a verdict on the hardware.
  • Turn off all background refresh, location, and notifications at once. You'd lose a lot of usefulness to fix a problem you haven't identified yet.
  • Copy someone's "most battery-saving settings" list. Their phone, their usage, their problem — probably not yours. The Battery page shows your actual causes.
  • Reset or restore the phone after an update. That creates more background work and usually makes things worse for a while, not better.
  • Assume the battery is dying because you saw a percentage somewhere. Battery Health "Service" is the official signal — check there, not on forums.

A simple way to decide

  • You just updated iOS or set up the phone → wait a few days, then compare the Daily Usage chart again. Most likely nothing to do.
  • Battery shows one app or system activity with a clear label (background, notifications, low signal) → change only the setting tied to that label, then compare a normal day.
  • Today was simply a heavy day — long calls, video, navigation, weak-signal travel, gaming → that's the phone doing real work. Nothing needs fixing.
  • Persistent drain, no visible cause, and Battery Health says Service → this is the point where checking with Apple Support makes sense, calmly and without panic.
