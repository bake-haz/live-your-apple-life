Why Does My iPhone Get Warm When I'm Not Using It?

A warm phone is usually just a busy phone — not a broken one.

You're probably here because you picked up your iPhone and it was warm even though the screen was off, or it was sitting on the table doing nothing obvious. It's natural to worry: phones aren't supposed to be little radiators. But in most cases the heat is a side effect of something the phone is finishing in the background — and Apple publishes a list of exactly when that's expected.
Direct answer: a warm iPhone is usually normal, and you don't need to do anything. Setup, a restore, a big software update, wireless charging, uploading photos to iCloud, a graphics-heavy game, or streaming video can all make the phone warm. It cools down on its own when the task ends. You only need to act if the phone shows a temperature warning, gets too hot to hold, or the screen goes dark and won't come back.

When warmth is simply normal

Apple's own support note says you may notice the device feels warmer when you do any of these. None of them means the phone is damaged:

  • First-time setup or signing in. Right after you turn it on or sign into your Apple Account, it's organizing, downloading, and indexing. That's work, and work makes heat.
  • Restoring from a backup. Moving your apps, photos, and messages back onto the phone takes real processing, especially from iCloud.
  • Finishing a software update. A major iOS update does the heaviest lifting after the progress bar finishes — it's rebuilding and re-indexing in the background.
  • Wireless charging. Charging on a pad generates more heat than a cable. A pad that isn't lined up perfectly generates even more.
  • Heavy or camera apps. Games, video editing, video calls, and the camera itself push the processor and can warm the phone.
  • Streaming high-quality video. Both decoding the video and sending it over the network create heat.
  • A warm room or direct sun. The phone is designed to run between about 0° and 35° C (32° to 95° F). Above that, it warms faster and may slow itself down to stay safe.

The note's exact reassurance is worth keeping in mind: "If your device doesn't display a temperature warning, you can keep using your device." Warm, but usable, is fine.

Why it feels warm "when you're not using it"

The screen being off doesn't mean the phone is idle. Several things keep running after you lock it:

  • Photo and file syncing. If you use iCloud Photos, the phone may still be uploading the day's photos, especially over Wi-Fi at night. Uploading a lot of video is warm work.
  • App updates and downloads. If automatic updates are on, the phone fetches new app versions and finishes installing them in the background. See Why does my iPhone seem to do things on its own?
  • Indexing after an update. For a while after a big iOS update, the phone rebuilds its search index and photo recognition. This is invisible but real.
  • Background activity from apps. Some apps refresh content, play audio, or use location while "closed." That's covered in Why does an app still seem to work after I closed it?

So "not using it" and "not doing anything" aren't the same. The heat is usually the tail end of a task you started earlier.

A simple way to tell normal from "let it cool"

Ask three questions. If the answer to all three is "no," you're in the normal zone:

  • Is there a temperature warning on screen? If the phone tells you it needs to cool down, that's the signal to stop and let it.
  • Is it too hot to hold comfortably? Warm is fine; painfully hot is the line where you set it down.
  • Did the screen go dark and refuse to wake? That's the phone protecting itself, and it returns to normal once it cools.

What to do when it's just warm

You usually don't need to do anything. If you'd rather it cool faster, a few low-effort steps help:

  • Set it down and let the task finish. Most warmth clears in minutes once the upload, update, or sync completes.
  • Take it off a wireless charger. A cable runs cooler, and the phone stops generating charging heat.
  • Move it out of sun or a hot car. Environmental heat is the easiest to remove.
  • Close a heavy app you left open. If a game or video editor is still on screen, leaving it open keeps the processor busy.

Notice what's not on this list: a factory reset, a battery replacement, or assuming the phone is defective. None of those are the first move for ordinary warmth.

When to actually check further

A few patterns are worth a closer look — not because the phone is "broken," but because they point at something specific:

  • It's always warm, even sitting idle for hours. If the phone never cools, something is running constantly. Check which apps use the most battery in Settings > Battery (see Why does my iPhone battery drop so fast after I get it?), and consider closing or offloading a runaway app.
  • Warmth comes with fast battery loss. Heat and drain together often mean a background task is looping. The same Battery screen shows you the culprit.
  • You see a "Charging On Hold" notice. That exact message means the phone paused charging to protect itself and will resume when it cools. It's the phone doing its job, not failing.
  • A temperature warning appears repeatedly in normal use. If it warns you while you're just texting in a normal room, that's worth a support check — but it's rare.

You probably don't need to worry about

  • Warmth during charging. Especially wireless charging. It's expected, not a defect.
  • A warm phone after a big update. The phone is finishing setup work for a while afterward. Normal.
  • Slight warmth in a pocket on a hot day. Body heat plus a warm environment adds up. Not a sign of trouble.
  • The phone feeling warm but working perfectly. No warning, no dark screen, no pain-to-hold = you're fine.
