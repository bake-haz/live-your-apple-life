My iPhone Is Lost or Stolen — What Should I Do First?

There is a right order for this, and it starts with finding — not panicking.

You're probably here because your iPhone isn't where you left it — maybe it slipped out of a pocket, maybe it's in the car, maybe someone took it. Your first instinct is to do everything at once. Don't. The steps below go in order, and each one only makes sense after the one before it.
Direct answer: try to locate it first with Find My — if you can't get it back, mark it as Lost, and if you believe it was stolen, change your Apple Account password. Concretely: (1) call it or retrace your steps if you think it's nearby; (2) open Find My on another device or at iCloud.com/find and see where it is; (3) if you can't recover it, tap Mark as Lost to lock it and keep tracking it; (4) if it was stolen, change your password at appleid.apple.com; (5) only erase it remotely after you've decided the phone isn't coming back. Do not erase first — Mark as Lost does more good (it locks the phone, keeps tracking it, and suspends Apple Pay), and erasing is irreversible. On devices running iOS 15 or later, Find My can still show the device's location after a remote erase — but you can't get the phone back that way, so erase stays a last resort.

What Find My can and can't do

Find My is your main tool, and knowing its limits keeps you calm and effective:

  • Locate. Shows the phone on a map if it's online. If it's offline, it may show the last known location, or nothing at all.
  • Play Sound. Makes it ring loudly even on silent — the best move when the phone is under a couch cushion or in the car.
  • Mark as Lost (Lost Mode). Locks the phone with your passcode, displays a message you write (like a phone number to call), and keeps tracking it. It also suspends Apple Pay on the device.
  • Erase. Wipes the phone remotely — the last resort, because it's irreversible. On devices running iOS 15 or later, Find My can still show the device's location after a remote erase, and Activation Lock keeps it locked to your Apple Account. Don't remove the device from Find My after erasing — removing it releases Activation Lock.

One honest limit: all of this only works if Find My was turned on before the phone went missing. If it was never enabled, you won't be able to locate it — skip to the data and account steps below.

The order that makes sense

  1. Misplaced, not lost? Call the phone, or use Play Sound if it's likely nearby. Most "lost" iPhones are in the car, under the bed, or with a family member.
  2. See it on the map but out of reach? Mark as Lost. The phone locks itself, and if someone honest finds it, they'll see your message with a way to reach you.
  3. Gone and you believe it was stolen? Change your Apple Account password now (appleid.apple.com), mark as Lost, then contact your carrier to suspend the line and consider a police report. A stolen phone that can't be unlocked is much less useful to a thief.
  4. You've decided it isn't coming back? Only then use Erase — as a data-protection step, and only after you've changed your password and confirmed your important data is backed up (see below). Erasing doesn't end Find My tracking on iOS 15 or later, but don't remove the device from Find My — that would release Activation Lock.

Your data and your Apple Account

The phone is replaceable; your data and account matter more. Do these in order:

  • Change your Apple Account password if there's any chance someone else can use the phone. Do it at appleid.apple.com from any device or browser.
  • Review the device list on your account. Remove the lost phone from it so it can't receive your verification codes, and remove anything you don't recognize — but keep the phone in Find My. Removing it from Find My releases Activation Lock.
  • Check that your photos, messages, and backups are safe. If you used iCloud Photos and iCloud Backup, your data is safe even though the phone is gone — see Is iCloud Photos actually a backup? for what that means, and Do you need iCloud Backup? for whether you were covered.
  • If your data wasn't backed up and you're worried about the gap, read What if my iPhone is lost before everything syncs? — it explains exactly what survived and what didn't.

What not to do

  • Don't erase first. Mark as Lost locks the phone and keeps tracking it, which is more useful than wiping it. Erase only when you've decided the phone isn't coming back — and even then, don't remove it from Find My afterward, because that releases Activation Lock.
  • Don't approve anything you didn't start. If the phone pops up later asking to confirm a login, that's not your phone "coming back" — someone else may have it. Change your password immediately and see is someone asking for my Apple ID code a scam? for the pattern.
  • Don't panic about your photos being gone forever. If iCloud Photos was on, they're on Apple's servers, not just on the missing phone.
  • Don't assume a thief can use it. Activation Lock keeps a lost or erased iPhone locked to your Apple Account. A locked phone is mostly worthless to a thief.

When to contact your carrier or the police

  • Carrier: if you believe the phone was stolen, contact them to suspend service on that line — it protects you from misuse and sometimes helps with recovery.
  • Police: report the theft if you have the serial number (find it on your original receipt or your Apple Account's device list). A police report is also what insurance or your carrier may ask for.

A simple way to decide

  • Probably nearby → Play Sound. Done, usually.
  • On the map, out of reach → Mark as Lost. It locks itself and waits.
  • Stolen → password change + Lost Mode + carrier + police. In that order.
  • Never coming back → erase, after everything above.

Every step has a calm, correct moment. The phone is gone; your data, your account, and your peace of mind don't have to be.

You probably don't need to

  • Buy a new phone in the first hour. Check Find My, the car, and Lost Mode first — recovery happens more often than panic suggests.
  • Pay a third party to "find" or "unlock" it. Find My and Apple Support are the only legitimate paths.
  • Assume your account is compromised because the phone is gone. Review your account security — check the trusted devices and phone numbers on it — and change your password if you see anything you don't recognize. That, plus removing the lost phone from your device list, is the effective response.
