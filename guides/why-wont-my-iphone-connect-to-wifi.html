Why Won't My iPhone Connect to Wi-Fi?

Nine times out of ten, it's the network or a switch — not the phone.

You're probably here because your iPhone either refuses to join your Wi-Fi, joins but shows no internet, or drops the connection a few minutes later. It feels personal, like the phone is being difficult. Usually it isn't. Wi-Fi is a conversation between the phone and a router (and sometimes your internet provider), and any one of those three can be the quiet cause.
Direct answer: before you assume the iPhone is broken, check the simple things. Make sure Airplane Mode is off, Wi-Fi is on, and you're in range of the router. If it still won't connect, "forget" the network and rejoin it. Most Wi-Fi problems end there. The phone is the last suspect, not the first.

What actually controls Wi-Fi on the iPhone

Open Settings > Wi-Fi. That screen is the whole control room: it shows whether Wi-Fi is on, which network you're joined to (a blue checkmark means connected), and the other networks nearby. Two things on that same phone quietly affect Wi-Fi:

  • Airplane Mode. If it's on, every radio is off — including Wi-Fi. You can turn Wi-Fi back on after enabling Airplane Mode, but if Airplane Mode is on and you forgot, the phone will look "unable to connect" when it's really just obeying you.
  • Wi-Fi Assist. In Settings > Cellular, near the bottom, there's a switch called Wi-Fi Assist. When it's on, the iPhone quietly uses cellular data if Wi-Fi seems too weak. That's handy, but it can make it feel like Wi-Fi "dropped" when really the phone switched to data. Turning it off makes Wi-Fi behavior more predictable.

Try these in order — they fix most cases

  1. Toggle Wi-Fi off and on. In Control Center (swipe down from the top-right) or Settings, turn it off, wait a few seconds, turn it on. Small, frequent, and surprisingly effective.
  2. Restart the iPhone. If the Wi-Fi setting is dimmed or the phone simply won't hold a connection, a restart clears the temporary state. Hold the side button and a volume button, then slide to power off.
  3. Move closer to the router. Walls, microwaves, and distance weaken the signal. Stand near the router and try again before changing anything else.
  4. "Forget" the network and rejoin. On the Wi-Fi screen, tap the network name, choose Forget This Network, then pick it again and enter the password. This clears a stuck or wrong saved connection — the single most common real fix.
  5. Check the password. A wrong password is the most ordinary reason a phone "won't connect." If someone changed the router password, the iPhone is still trying the old one.

When the problem is the network, not the phone

If your iPhone won't connect but your laptop or another phone connects fine to the same Wi-Fi, the issue is probably the router or the internet connection, not your iPhone. Useful checks:

  • Other devices on the same Wi-Fi can reach the internet? If not, the problem is upstream — restart the router and modem by unplugging them for a few seconds.
  • The network is hidden or managed by someone else (work, school, a café). Some networks block unknown devices or need a separate sign-in page. In that case the iPhone is behaving correctly; you need the network's owner.
  • VPN or security software. A VPN profile can reroute or block Wi-Fi. If you installed one, removing it and restarting can suddenly bring Wi-Fi back.

When it connects but keeps dropping

A connection that comes and goes is usually the router side, not the iPhone. Two ordinary causes: the phone is roaming between the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands of the same network name (some routers "band-steer" automatically and do it clumsily), and distance — 5 GHz is faster but dies quickly through walls. If drops happen only at the edge of the house, that's signal, not a fault. Try joining the other band if your router shows separate names, or move the router more central. A router that hasn't been restarted in months is also a common, boring culprit; unplugging it for ten seconds clears stale connection state that no phone setting can fix.

Public Wi-Fi and the "sign-in" page

At a café, hotel, or airport, joining the network is only half — many then show a browser sign-in page before internet works. If Safari opens to a "wi-fi" login page, that's expected; complete it. If you don't see it, open Safari and load any plain page and the prompt usually appears. This is a network rule, not an iPhone limitation, and it's why "connected but no internet" at a coffee shop is normal until you sign in.

You probably don't need to

Don't jump to "my iPhone is defective" or buy a new router on day one. And don't reset everything on the phone as a first move — there's a more targeted step below. The phone connecting fine on a different Wi-Fi (a friend's, a café's) already tells you the hardware is likely fine.

The deeper fix: Reset Network Settings

If the simple steps fail and the phone connects nowhere, there's one clean reset that often helps: Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Reset > Reset Network Settings. This removes all saved Wi-Fi networks and passwords, cellular settings, and VPN/APN configurations, then lets the phone rebuild them fresh. It does not delete your photos, apps, or messages — only network settings. You'll re-enter Wi-Fi passwords afterward. Use it when nothing else has worked, and skip it if a school or business set up your network (ask their IT first).

A simple way to decide

If the iPhone connects to other Wi-Fi but not yours → the problem is your router or network. If it connects to no Wi-Fi anywhere → restart, then Reset Network Settings. That one question saves most people an unnecessary trip to a repair shop.
