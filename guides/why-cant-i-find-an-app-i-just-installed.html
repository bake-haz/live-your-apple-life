Why Can't I Find an App I Just Installed?

A missing icon and a failed install look identical from the Home Screen. They are not the same thing.

You're probably here because the download finished, you went back to your Home Screen, and the icon wasn't there. That's an unsettling moment for a specific reason: the Home Screen is the place you've been trained to trust, and it just appeared to tell you that nothing happened. What's worth knowing before you decide anything is that the Home Screen is not where your iPhone keeps its record of what's installed. It's a layout — one that both you and iOS edit. So the thing that's missing might be the icon rather than the app. The rest of this page is about telling those two apart, because the fix for one is nothing like the fix for the other.
Direct answer: "not on my Home Screen" and "not installed" are two different findings, and the Home Screen alone can't tell you which one you have. An app can be fully installed and still show no icon — because it sits in the App Library, because it landed on a page you haven't swiped to, or because your iPhone is set to keep new downloads off the Home Screen. It is also possible the download never finished. Three checks settle it: what the App Store says, what Search returns, and what the App Library shows.

The first judgment: installed, or just not visible?

Before hunting through pages of icons, gather evidence from the three places that actually know.

  • What the App Store says. Open the App Store, search the app by name, and look at the button next to it. If it says Open, the App Store considers the app installed on this iPhone. If it still offers Get, or shows a cloud icon or a progress indicator, that's a different answer and it matters.
  • What Search returns. From the Home Screen, swipe down from the middle of the screen, or tap the Search field near the bottom, and type part of the app's name. If the app appears and opens, it is on your iPhone.
  • What the App Library shows. Swipe left past your last Home Screen page to reach the App Library. It lists your installed apps grouped into categories, with a search field at the top.

Any one of those three showing the app is stronger evidence than an empty Home Screen is. All three coming up empty is a genuinely different result, and it deserves the closer look described further down.

The Home Screen and the App Library are not the same list

The App Library is the complete, automatic collection of the apps on your iPhone. The Home Screen is an arrangement you curate: you move icons, you remove icons, and iOS follows a setting when it decides where a new download goes.

That setting is in Settings > Home Screen & App Library, under Newly Downloaded Apps. It offers Add to Home Screen or App Library Only. If App Library Only is selected, a completely successful install produces no new Home Screen icon at all — that's the setting working as chosen, not an install failing. If Add to Home Screen is selected, the icon goes to the first open space, which can be a later page you had no reason to swipe to.

This is why the empty spot you were staring at is such poor evidence: two very different situations produce exactly the same view.

What Search can and can't prove

Search is the fastest check, and it has three distinct outcomes worth reading carefully.

  • Search finds it and it opens. The app is installed. What you were missing was its placement on the Home Screen layout you happened to be looking at. Nothing is wrong with the app.
  • Search finds nothing, but the app is still installed. iOS lets you move an app into a locked Hidden folder at the bottom of the App Library. Apps kept there don't appear in Search or in the normal App Library categories, and opening that folder requires Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode. So an empty search result does not close the question by itself.
  • Search finds nothing and neither does anything else. If Search is empty, the App Library has no trace of it including the Hidden folder, and the App Store still offers Get, then you're no longer looking at a placement question. That's the case to investigate rather than the case to rearrange.

The smallest action worth taking

If you found the app and you want it on the Home Screen, that's a short job. In the App Library, touch and hold the app, then choose Add to Home Screen. You can also touch and hold it and drag it out onto a Home Screen page. Either way the app itself is untouched; you're only creating the shortcut you expected to see.

If you'd like every future download to land on the Home Screen, change the one setting named above and leave everything else alone. That is the entire repair. There's no need to reorganize your pages, and no need to reinstall anything to make an icon appear.

When it deserves a closer look

Some results point at something other than placement. Any of these is worth following up:

  • The App Store doesn't treat it as installed. It still offers Get, or shows a cloud or a stalled progress indicator instead of Open.
  • An icon exists but looks unfinished. A dimmed icon with a progress ring, or one that does nothing when tapped, describes a download that stopped partway rather than one that completed.
  • Search and the App Library both come up empty, including the Hidden folder. At that point placement has been ruled out.
  • It opens, then behaves unexpectedly. If it closes immediately, or opens to something that doesn't match what you thought you installed, it's worth confirming you installed the app you meant and that you're signed in with the Apple Account you used to get it.

These are reasons to check your connection, your Apple Account, and the App Store's own view of the app. They aren't reasons to reset anything.

Before you blame yourself

A missing icon is weak evidence. It's equally consistent with a finished install, an unfinished install, a setting you never deliberately chose, a page you didn't swipe to, and an app someone moved into the Hidden folder. That's precisely why it cannot tell you what happened on its own.

Whatever you did or didn't tap, the Home Screen was never built to report install status — it reports layout. So the useful move is to treat the empty spot as a question rather than a verdict, and let the App Store, Search, and the App Library answer it. Those three know; the gap where you expected an icon does not.

You probably don't need to

Delete the app and reinstall it before you've checked whether it's already there. Reset your iPhone, or wipe your Home Screen layout, over a single icon. Change a handful of settings at once and lose track of which one did what. Conclude that the phone is broken. And most of all, treat Home Screen placement as if it were install status — that's the assumption doing the real damage here, because it turns a layout detail into an emergency.

A simple way to decide

If the App Store says Open and Search finds the app: it's installed — add it to the Home Screen if that's where you want it. If Search finds nothing but the App Store says Open: look through the App Library, including the Hidden folder. If the App Store still offers Get or shows a download indicator: treat it as an install that hasn't finished, check your connection and Apple Account, and let it complete. If nothing appears anywhere: that's the one branch worth investigating as an actual problem rather than a misplaced icon.
