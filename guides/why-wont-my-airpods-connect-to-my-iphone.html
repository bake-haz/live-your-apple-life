Why Won't My AirPods Connect to My iPhone?

Nine times out of ten it's battery, the wrong device, or a stale pairing — not broken hardware.

You're probably here because your AirPods worked yesterday and today they won't connect — or only one side connects, or they connect but play no sound. Before you assume they're broken (or worse, start shopping for new ones), run this check in order. It resolves most cases in about five minutes.
Direct answer: check battery, check which device they're connected to, then re-pair — in that order. Most "won't connect" AirPods are out of battery, silently connected to your Mac or iPad instead, or stuck on an old pairing. If they're charged, Bluetooth is on, and they're not claimed by another device, the fix is usually "forget" + re-pair, and if that fails, a reset of the AirPods themselves. Hardware failure is the last thing to suspect, not the first.

The five-minute check order

  1. Are they charged? Put both AirPods in the case, close the lid for a few seconds, then open it near your unlocked iPhone — a popup shows battery level. A dead earbud won't connect.
  2. Is Bluetooth on? Open Settings > Bluetooth and confirm it's enabled. If it's off, that's the whole mystery.
  3. Are they connected to another device? AirPods switch automatically between your Apple devices. If your Mac or iPad is nearby and grabbed them, your iPhone shows them as unavailable. Open Settings > Bluetooth on the iPhone and check what it says under your AirPods.
  4. Do they appear in the Bluetooth list? With the case open and the AirPods inside, they should show under Settings > Bluetooth. If they do but won't connect, go to the next section.
  5. If nothing shows, try the case trick: put the AirPods back in the case, close the lid, wait about 30 seconds, then open it again near the iPhone. This clears a sleeping connection more often than you'd think.

They show in Bluetooth but won't connect

This is the classic "stale pairing" — the iPhone remembers the AirPods, but the connection handshake is stuck. The fix is clean and quick:

  1. In Settings > Bluetooth, tap the "i" next to your AirPods and choose Forget This Device.
  2. Put both AirPods back in the case, close the lid, and wait a few seconds.
  3. Open the lid near the iPhone — the setup animation appears and they pair fresh.

This clears the bad connection without losing anything. It's the single most common real fix.

They don't show up at all

  • Make sure they're charged (back to step one — a completely dead case charges nothing).
  • Keep the lid open with the AirPods inside while you look at the Bluetooth list; that's how they broadcast.
  • If the case is older-style with a button (first-generation AirPods cases), pressing the button on the back puts them in pairing mode.

Only one side connects

  • That earbud may be out of battery. Check the per-earbud level in the popup or in Settings > Bluetooth > your AirPods.
  • Put both in the case together for a minute so the low one charges a little, then try again.
  • If it still won't connect, forget and re-pair (steps above) — a half-broken pairing is a common one-side cause.

The reset, when the simple stuff fails

If forget-and-re-pair didn't fix it, reset the AirPods themselves (this clears their settings and pairings):

  1. Put both AirPods in the case and close the lid.
  2. Wait about 30 seconds, then open the lid.
  3. On the iPhone, go to Settings > Bluetooth, tap the "i" next to your AirPods, and tap Forget This Device.
  4. With the lid still open, press and hold the button on the back of the case for about 15 seconds, until the light flashes amber, then white.
  5. Keep the lid open near the iPhone — the setup animation should appear. Pair like new.

The exact button placement and light colors vary slightly by AirPods model, but the principle is the same on all of them.

When it's actually the hardware

You've done all of the above — charged, re-paired, reset — and the AirPods still won't connect, or they connect to your other devices but not this iPhone:

  • Try them with another Apple device. If they connect fine to a friend's iPhone or your iPad, the AirPods are fine and the issue is this iPhone's pairing — a fresh re-pair or an iOS update usually clears it.
  • If they connect to nothing anywhere, that points at the AirPods themselves. This is when Apple Support is the right next step, and it's worth checking whether they're still in warranty.

A simple way to decide

One question separates most cases: do the AirPods connect to any other device? If yes → the AirPods are fine; fix the iPhone pairing (forget + re-pair, then reset if needed). If no → the problem is the AirPods (battery, then hardware). And if the whole thing started after an iOS update, check for a newer update — Why does my iPhone keep asking me to update? explains why updates quietly fix connection bugs.

You probably don't need to

  • Buy new AirPods on day one. The overwhelming majority of connection problems are fixed by charge, re-pair, or reset.
  • Reset first thing. Reset is a good second or third step, not a first one — the simple checks fix more cases with less fuss.
  • Blame your iPhone for everything. A wrong-device connection (your Mac grabbed them) looks exactly like "iPhone won't connect" — check what they're actually connected to first.
  • Panic about losing your pairing. Forgetting and re-pairing is routine and loses nothing.
