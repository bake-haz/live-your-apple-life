Why Won't My AirPods Connect to My iPhone?

They worked yesterday. Today you open the case, put them in, and the iPhone acts as if nothing happened. That doesn't tell you the AirPods are dead — it tells you the connection state changed.

You're probably here because your AirPods connected fine before, and now they don't — or only one side connects, or they connect but the sound comes from the phone. The symptom is binary (sound or no sound), which makes it feel like a hardware failure. Before assuming that, you can find out which side of the connection the evidence points to.
Direct answer: an AirPods connection problem can come from the AirPods, the iPhone, or the connection state between them. Start by finding out whether the iPhone can see the AirPods and whether another device is involved. Then match the symptom to the right check:
  • AirPods aren't visible at all — that's a discovery or pairing-state problem.
  • AirPods are visible but won't connect — the iPhone sees them, so the issue is the pairing or connection state, not discovery.
  • AirPods connect but no sound comes through them — an audio-output routing problem, not necessarily a pairing failure.
  • Only one side behaves differently — a per-earbud charging or recognition issue, not the same as both failing.
  • They fail across multiple devices — the evidence points more strongly toward the AirPods or the case.

First, describe the failure

Before tapping anything, name the exact symptom. Different symptoms point at different parts of the connection.

AirPods don't appear on the iPhone

This is a discovery or pairing-state problem. Confirm the AirPods are in the open case with charge, Bluetooth is on, and the case is in pairing mode. AirPods 4 and AirPods Pro 3 enter pairing mode with a front-of-case double-tap; other current models use the back setup button.

AirPods appear but won't connect

The iPhone can see them, so discovery isn't the whole problem. Look at the pairing state, whether another device holds the active connection, and a reconnect or forget-and-reconnect.

AirPods connect but no sound comes through them

This is an audio-output routing question, not necessarily a Bluetooth pairing failure. Check which device is selected as the audio destination on the iPhone, then reassess. It is a short check, not a full audio-routing tutorial.

Only one AirPod behaves differently

This differs from both earbuds failing. Confirm both are charging, check the per-earbud battery state, and reinsert both in the case to reassess. Don't call it hardware failure yet.

They fail with multiple devices

If the same failure happens on every device you try, the evidence points more strongly toward the AirPods or the charging case. Use evidence, not certainty.

The order Apple suggests

Apple's current steps run in this order: update the device to the latest software; put both AirPods in the case and confirm charging; make sure Bluetooth is on; check whether they appear and can be selected; close the lid, reopen, and place the case near the device to reconnect; then reset if they still won't connect. Use it as a reference.

Reset is a later step, not the first

Reset clears the connection state when simpler checks fail. Apple places it after those checks, not before, and it doesn't repair hardware or fix an iPhone-side problem. Treat it as a state-clearing step.

How to reset — by model

Reset steps differ by model (the name shows in Settings > Bluetooth next to your AirPods).

  • AirPods 1, 2, 3 and AirPods Pro 1, 2 (setup button on the back): with both in the case and the lid open, press and hold the back button for about 15 seconds, until the light flashes amber, then white.
  • AirPods 4 (all models) and AirPods Pro 3 (no setup button): with the lid open, double-tap the front of the case when the status light comes on, double-tap again when it flashes white, and double-tap a third time when it flashes faster — until the light flashes amber, then white.

When the AirPods are listed, Apple also has you remove the pairing on the iPhone (Settings > Bluetooth > info button > Forget This Device) before the case step, then pair like new near the iPhone. Apple's support page lists the exact steps for your model.

They may be connected to another Apple device

AirPods can link to several Apple devices, and automatic switching can move the active connection. If your Mac or iPad is nearby, it may hold it — check the audio destination selected on the iPhone before assuming the AirPods are unavailable. It isn't a rule that your Mac "grabbed" them; the connection lives somewhere you can move it.

Is it Bluetooth, or is it the AirPods?

This page isn't a general Bluetooth guide — see Why won't my Bluetooth device connect to my iPhone? for that. Use this split:

  • Another Bluetooth accessory also won't connect → the iPhone-side Bluetooth path deserves more attention.
  • Other Bluetooth devices connect but the AirPods don't → focus on the AirPods, their pairing state, charging state, or model-specific reset.
  • The AirPods fail everywhere → the evidence points more toward the AirPods/case side.

What the connection test tells you

A cross-device test changes where the evidence points, not proof a component is "fine."

  • AirPods connect to another Apple device → the iPhone side becomes more worth investigating.
  • AirPods don't connect to another device either → the AirPods/case side deserves closer inspection.
  • Another Bluetooth accessory works with the iPhone → a broad iPhone Bluetooth failure is less supported by the evidence.
  • Multiple Bluetooth devices fail on the iPhone → the iPhone-side Bluetooth or network state deserves attention.

What "Forget This Device" does and doesn't do

Forget This Device removes the pairing so the two can pair again. It is not a factory reset, not a hardware repair, and not a guarantee the next pairing works. After one clean cycle, move to the next step rather than repeating it.

Make sure both AirPods are actually charging

One earbud can charge differently from the other. Put both in the case and confirm each shows charging rather than assuming the case has power. Don't invent a minimum percentage — the check is whether each earbud is recognized and charging.

When the evidence points toward the AirPods

Evidence — not a guess — is what moves you here:

  • neither earbud reliably connects across multiple devices;
  • the case doesn't behave as expected during pairing or reset;
  • one earbud stays consistently unrecognized after the supported checks;
  • charging behavior itself looks abnormal.

At that point Apple Support or service is a reasonable next step. Don't declare hardware failure without that evidence.

Before you decide they're broken

The failure feels like hardware because the symptom is binary: sound or no sound. But the path between your iPhone and AirPods has separate states — discovery, pairing, connection, audio output. You don't need to guess which failed; you can narrow it down by checking each.

You probably don't need to

  • Buy new AirPods because they failed to connect once. A single failed connection is not evidence the set is dead.
  • Reset the AirPods before confirming charge and Bluetooth. Reset is a later step, after the simpler checks.
  • Forget and re-pair repeatedly. One clean cycle is the test; repeating it doesn't add information.
  • Change iPhone-wide settings before testing another Bluetooth device. A cross-device test is a cheaper way to locate the problem.
  • Assume one missing earbud response means the whole set is dead. One earbud and the pair are different failure scopes.
  • Assume another device appearing in the process means your AirPods are defective. It may simply hold the active connection.

A simple way to decide

  • They don't appear at all. → check charge, case state, Bluetooth, and pairing mode.
  • They appear but won't connect. → inspect the pairing state and test the connection path.
  • They connect but sound comes from the iPhone. → treat it as an audio output / route question.
  • Only one side behaves differently. → check each earbud's charging and recognition state.
  • They work on another device. → the evidence points more toward this iPhone or its connection state.
  • They fail on multiple devices. → the evidence points more toward the AirPods/case side.
  • Nothing makes sense. → stop changing settings randomly; identify the exact symptom, then choose the next supported step.
