Do I really need a case and a screen protector?

Not strictly. It depends on how you carry and use the phone — not on a rule everyone must follow.

You're probably here because you just got a new iPhone and everyone — the store, the internet, well-meaning friends — says you "need" a case and a screen protector or you'll regret it. You're not sure if that's real protection or just pressure to buy. You'd like a straight answer.
Direct answer: you don't have to use either one. A case mainly reduces the risk of cracked glass if you drop the phone; a screen protector mainly reduces scratches on the glass. Both are useful for some people and unnecessary for others. The phone is built to be used without them. What matters is your own situation — how you carry it, who else touches it, and how bothered you'd be by a scratch. You definitely don't need the most expensive version of either.

What each one actually does

They protect against different things, so the question isn't "case or not" in the abstract — it's "which risks apply to me":

  • A case adds a layer around the edges and back. Its main job is to absorb the shock of a drop, especially onto a hard surface, where the glass is most likely to crack. A thicker case generally cushions more; a thin one mostly guards against scuffs on the frame.
  • A screen protector is a thin film or glass layer over the display. Its main job is to take scratches — from keys in a pocket, a fingernail, grit in a bag — so the scratches land on the cheap layer, not the real screen.

Neither makes the phone indestructible. A case lowers drop risk; a protector lowers scratch risk. That's the honest size of what they do.

How to decide for your life

Run the question against how you actually use the phone, not against what a salesperson would say:

  • You carry it loose in a pocket or bag with keys. A screen protector earns its keep here — that's exactly how screens get scratched.
  • You have young kids, or clumsy hands, or you're often on the move. A case is the bigger win, because drops are the main way glass cracks.
  • It lives on a desk or in a clean pocket, and you're careful. You may be perfectly happy with neither, or just a thin case. Plenty of careful people go caseless.
  • You plan to sell or trade it in later. A case (and protector) keeps the body pristine, which helps resale value. If that matters to you, protection pays off twice.

You probably don't need to

  • Buy the priciest option. A basic case cushions drops about as well as a fancy one for most real falls. The expensive ones sell grip, looks, and brand — not dramatically better safety.
  • Use both "just in case." If scratches don't bother you and you don't drop things, a case alone (or nothing) is a reasonable choice. More gear isn't automatically safer.
  • Panic that the screen will shatter tomorrow. Modern iPhone glass is tough. A case reduces a real risk; it doesn't represent a countdown to disaster without one.
  • Skip the phone's own safety habits. Where you put it matters more than what you wrap it in — a phone on a stable surface beats an armored phone sliding off a car roof.

Keeping it clean (no gear required)

Whether or not you use a case, the screen stays nicest if you clean it the safe way. Apple's general guidance is simple and free:

  • Unplug cables and turn the phone off before wiping, if you can.
  • Use a soft, lint-free cloth — the kind you'd use on glasses.
  • For fingerprints or smudges, slightly dampen the cloth with plain water and wipe gently. A dry cloth handles most dust.
  • Avoid window cleaners, bleach, abrasives, or aerosol sprays. They can damage the coating on the glass and the seal around the phone.

You don't need a special "screen cleaning kit." A clean microfiber cloth and a little water do the job, and they won't harm the display or a protector you've added.

If you do buy one, keep it simple

Should you choose to, the calm path is:

  • Pick a case that fits your model exactly and doesn't block the side buttons or camera. A snug, simple case protects as well as a complicated one.
  • For a screen protector, a basic tempered-glass one is enough. Fancy claims about "military grade" don't change the everyday math — it's a scratch barrier you can replace for a few dollars.
  • Apply it in a low-dust spot so you don't trap a speck under the glass. A slightly imperfect edge is normal and harmless.

Notice none of this requires a brand name or a premium price. The point is cushion and cover, not status.

A simple way to decide

Picture your worst realistic day: phone slips from a pocket, or rides with keys in a bag. If that image makes you wince, get a case (and a protector if it's going near keys). If it barely registers — you're careful and the phone has a calm home — you can skip one or both and enjoy the phone as designed. Either answer is fine; only "I bought both out of fear and never liked the feel" is a small regret.
