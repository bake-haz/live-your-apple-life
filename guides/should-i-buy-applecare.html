Should I Buy AppleCare+ for My iPhone?

AppleCare+ is insurance for repairs — not a promise that your phone will break, and not a requirement for owning one.

You're probably here because you saw the AppleCare+ pitch at checkout, or the reminder in Settings, and you're not sure whether it's a smart safety net or a clever upsell. The price feels real, the fear feels real, and "just add it" feels like the easy answer. You'd like to decide with your head, not your nerves.
Direct answer: AppleCare+ is an extended repair and accidental-damage plan sold by Apple — and whether it's worth it comes down to three things: how long you keep a phone, how hard you are on it, and whether a repair bill would genuinely hurt you. If you've damaged a phone before or you plan to keep this one for years, it's usually a reasonable buy. If you're careful and tend to upgrade within a couple of years, you can confidently skip it. There is no "must buy" — only a math problem about your own habits.

What AppleCare+ actually is

Strip away the brochure language and it's a straightforward repair plan:

  • It extends your warranty beyond the standard limited warranty, for repairs and service on your iPhone.
  • It covers accidental damage — like a cracked screen or water damage — for a per-incident service fee. That fee is usually much smaller than paying for the repair outright.
  • It covers battery service if your battery's capacity drops below 80% while the plan is active.
  • In some countries, theft and loss coverage is available as an extra option that replaces a lost or stolen phone (subject to a fee and availability).

You can typically buy it upfront for a multi-year term or pay monthly, depending on your country and device. Availability, prices, and fees vary by region and model — so treat any number you see online as "a number, in a place, at a time," not a universal fact.

What it is not

  • A replacement for a case. The plan covers the cost of damage after it happens; a case reduces the chance of damage in the first place. They solve different problems, and many people sensibly use a case and skip the plan.
  • Maintenance. It doesn't clean your phone, optimize it, or make it faster. It's for when something breaks.
  • A guarantee you'll ever use it. Most people who buy it never file a claim. That's not a sign it was useless — it's how insurance works — but it does mean the money is a bet on your own future clumsiness.

The three factors that actually decide it

Run your own situation through these:

  • How long do you keep a phone? AppleCare+ spans years, not months. If you upgrade every one to two years, you're paying for coverage you mostly won't use. If you keep phones until they die — three, four, five years — a plan that covers years three and four is much more valuable.
  • How hard are you on devices? Be honest, not aspirational. Have you cracked a screen, dropped a phone in the last two years, or do you regularly use it around water, kids, or busy commutes? Past behavior is the best predictor. If your answer is yes, the plan is worth more to you.
  • Would a repair bill hurt? A cracked-screen repair is expensive. If that cost would genuinely stress your budget, the plan converts an unpredictable bill into a predictable one. If you could absorb it without flinching, the plan is less necessary.

A simple way to decide

Two questions cover most people:

  • Have you damaged a phone in the last two years? Yes → the peace of mind is probably worth the price. No → read on.
  • Do you plan to keep this phone for 3+ years? Yes → the plan covers the risky years when it's old and out of warranty. No → you're likely paying for coverage you'll barely use.

If your answer is "no" to both — careful with phones, upgrades every couple of years — skipping AppleCare+ is a calm, defensible choice. If you're unsure, you don't have to decide at the checkout counter: you usually have a window after purchase to add it, and you can check your eligibility in Settings > General > AppleCare & Warranty (the exact path can vary slightly by iOS version).

You probably don't need to

  • Buy it out of panic at the register. The pitch is designed to make the moment feel urgent. You usually have time to think it over.
  • Assume it's required. It isn't. The phone works identically with or without it.
  • Skip a case because you have the plan. They're different layers of protection, and a case saves you the deductible, the downtime, and the hassle — not just the money.
  • Buy it for a phone you'll trade in within a year. If the phone is leaving your hands soon, you're paying for coverage the next owner will benefit from. The value doesn't neatly transfer with the phone.
