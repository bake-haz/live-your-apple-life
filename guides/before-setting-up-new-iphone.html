What Should I Do Before Setting Up My New iPhone?

For the few minutes before you open the box — the part that saves the most headaches later.

You're probably here because a new iPhone just arrived — or it's on the way — and part of you wants to open the box, sign in, and finally clear off the old phone that's been nagging you about storage. That instinct is normal. This page is about the short pause before that, which is where most avoidable problems get prevented.
Don't wipe, sell, or trade in the old iPhone yet. First make sure your Apple Account, password, a recent backup, and enough iCloud space are in place. Then move your data — and only erase the old phone after you've confirmed the new one actually has everything. A few minutes of checking now is far easier than recovering something once it's gone.

Before you start

None of these are complicated. They're just worth confirming while the old phone is still in your hand:

  • The old phone still works and has some charge. You'll likely need it nearby during setup, or to make a backup first.
  • Your Apple Account and password. That's the email or phone number plus password you use for iCloud, the App Store, and Find My. You'll sign in on the new phone — if you've forgotten it, sort that out now, not halfway through setup.
  • Two-factor / a trusted device or number. Signing in to a new phone often asks you to approve from another device or enter a code. Have that handy.
  • Wi-Fi. Setup and transfers happen over Wi-Fi; a steady network avoids mid-transfer restarts.
  • A little power (or a cable). Keep both phones charged during a transfer so neither dies halfway.
  • A recent backup. iCloud Backup turned on, or a fresh computer backup — your safety net if anything goes sideways.
  • iCloud storage room. If you plan to use iCloud to move data, check you have enough space (the free tier is 5 GB, and photos and backups add up quickly).

Decide how you want to move

There's no single "best" method — it depends on what you have in hand and how much you trust your current backup. Apple's own setup steps cover the exact on-screen taps; here's the thinking behind each choice.

Quick Start (old phone to new phone)

Place the two iPhones near each other and the new one offers to copy from the old one wirelessly — settings, accounts, apps, and most of your data follow along. Both need to be on Wi-Fi with Bluetooth on, and the old phone unlocked and close by.

+ Fast, and keeps almost everything in place with the least effort.

– Needs the old phone present and working; very large libraries can take a while.

iCloud Backup / iCloud data

Set up the new phone, sign in with your Apple Account, and restore from an iCloud Backup — or let Photos and Messages sync in from iCloud. Good when the old phone isn't available, or you're moving across a distance.

+ No computer needed; works even if the old phone is already gone.

– Depends on having a current backup and enough iCloud storage; the first sync can take time on a slower network.

Computer backup (Mac or PC)

Back up the old phone with Finder on a Mac or the Apple Devices app on Windows; choose "Encrypt" to also include passwords and Health data. Then restore that backup onto the new phone.

+ Local and complete, and not dependent on iCloud storage.

– You need the computer and a cable, and it's easy to forget to do it.

The things worth checking before you start

Setup moves what's on the phone or in your account. A quick mental checklist helps you notice anything that might not follow:

  • Photos. Are they in iCloud Photos, or only stored on the device? Photos that live only on the device won't arrive via an iCloud restore unless they were backed up.
  • Contacts and calendars. Usually in iCloud, but worth a glance to confirm they aren't stored "On My iPhone" only.
  • Messages. If Messages in iCloud is off, your message history lives on the device or backup — confirm it's included in whatever you're moving.
  • Important apps and their data. Banking, notes, chat apps with local history, fitness — some keep their own data and may need a re-login or their own backup.
  • Accounts and authentication. Authenticator apps, password managers, and email accounts often need re-verification after a move, so have recovery details ready.
  • Cellular / eSIM. Know whether your plan is eSIM (it can transfer during setup or through your carrier) or a physical SIM (you'll move the card). Don't cancel the old line until the new one is active.

After the transfer

  • Keep the old iPhone for now. Don't erase or trade it yet — it's your fallback if something didn't come across.
  • Open the things that matter. On the new phone, check Photos, Contacts, Messages, Mail, and a couple of key apps to confirm your stuff is there.
  • Confirm it actually works. Make sure calls, texts, and iMessage activate, and that you can receive a verification code if an app asks for one.
  • Give it a day. Some things — like a full photo library — finish downloading in the background. Live with the new phone a little before letting go of the old one.

When is it safe to let go of the old iPhone?

You can let go once all three are true: (1) the new phone has your photos, contacts, and messages; (2) you can sign in to your important accounts and receive codes; (3) you've lived with it for a day and nothing's missing. Only then sign out of the old phone (Settings › your name › Sign Out) and erase it (Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Erase All Content and Settings). If you're selling or giving it away, erasing also turns off Find My and Activation Lock so the next person can use it.

You probably don't need to worry about

Apps re-download themselves from the App Store once you sign in — you don't copy them by hand. Most of your settings, wallpaper, and layout come across with Quick Start or a backup. iOS updates on its own schedule, so you don't need the "right" version before moving. Find My and Activation Lock follow your Apple Account, not the device — just sign out and erase properly at the end. And you don't need to buy any accessory or software to move your data safely.

What makes sense for different people

  • A straightforward upgrade: use Quick Start, confirm the basics arrived, then erase the old phone after a day.
  • You have a lot of data: make an encrypted computer backup as a belt-and-suspenders safety net alongside iCloud, and start the transfer with time to spare.
  • Photos matter most to you: confirm iCloud Photos is on and fully synced before erasing; if your photos are device-only, back them up first.
  • You want to re-organize while switching: move everything first and get the new phone working, then tidy — don't try to declutter and migrate at the same time.
