What Should I Do Before Setting Up My New iPhone?

Setup finishing isn't the sign to erase the old iPhone. Being able to replace it is.

You're here because the new iPhone is ready and the old one is still sitting next to it — and you don't quite trust yourself to erase it yet. The problem isn't copying the data. The problem is knowing when the copy is complete enough to let go. The old phone is your safety net, and safety nets don't get thrown away on a schedule.
Direct answer: don't erase the old iPhone when setup merely says it's finished. Erase it when the new iPhone has taken over the things you personally depend on — your photos, your messages, your contacts, calls and texts, the accounts you sign in to, your authentication, and the apps and data that matter to you. The standard is replacement, not completion percentage. When the new phone can stand in for the old one in your real day, the old phone has done its job.

Before you start

A short preparation list prevents the avoidable kind of trouble. Confirm these while the old phone is still in your hand:

  • The old iPhone is available and charged. Quick Start needs it nearby; a backup needs it working.
  • Your Apple Account sign-in details. You'll sign in on the new phone — if you've forgotten the password, sort that out before setup begins.
  • A trusted device or phone number for the verification code. Signing in to a new phone asks you to verify; keep the way to receive that code handy.
  • Steady Wi-Fi and power on both phones. Transfers run over Wi-Fi, and neither phone should die mid-move.
  • A transfer path that fits how you want to move — Quick Start, an iCloud restore, or a computer backup. Not every method depends on extra iCloud storage; Quick Start and a computer backup don't.
  • Awareness of your carrier and eSIM situation. Whether your line can move depends on your carrier and on current device support, so don't let the old line go until the new one works.

Three ways to move

There's no universal "best" method — the right one depends on what's in your hand and how you want to move. Apple's setup screens show the exact taps; here's what each path is for.

Quick Start (iPhone to iPhone)

Old phone available and working? With the two iPhones near each other, both on Wi-Fi, the old one unlocked, Quick Start moves your settings, accounts, and data directly from one phone to the other. How long it takes depends on how much you're moving.

+ Direct, with little to do by hand.

– Needs the old phone present; very large libraries take time.

iCloud restore or sync

Set up the new phone and restore from an iCloud backup, or let content already kept in iCloud arrive through sync. Fits when the new phone needs to restore from iCloud, or when your photos and messages are already synced there.

+ No computer needed; works if the old phone is already gone.

– Depends on a current backup and on enough iCloud storage for that method.

Computer backup (Mac or PC)

Back up the old phone with Finder on a Mac or the Apple Devices app on Windows — choosing Encrypt to also include passwords and Health data — then restore that backup onto the new phone.

+ Local, and not dependent on iCloud storage.

– Needs the computer and a cable, and it's easy to forget to do it.

What can look finished even when your life hasn't fully moved yet

Setup can reach the Home Screen while parts of your life are still settling. That isn't a failed transfer — it's worth knowing what can still be arriving:

  • Apps may still be downloading after the Home Screen appears.
  • Cloud content can keep syncing — a full photo library, for example, continues arriving in the background.
  • Some apps need their own sign-in — banking and email included — even when the app itself has arrived.
  • Authentication needs attention — authenticator and password-manager flows may ask you to re-verify on the new device.
  • Carrier and eSIM activation needs verifying — the line isn't working until calls and data actually work on the new phone.
  • App-specific and locally stored data follows its own rules — chat history kept on-device, or notes inside an app with its own account, may need that app's own step.

None of these necessarily means something is missing. They mean "setup finished" and "everything is settled" are different moments.

The replacement test

This is the question that decides when the old phone can go:

If the old iPhone stopped working today, would I still have to rely on it for something?

If the answer is no, the old phone is no longer your operational dependency and can move toward erasing. If the answer is yes, name exactly what you still need from it, and resolve that before letting it go.

Work through the parts of your life that actually matter:

  • Photos. Can you open the photos you care about, and is the library present or syncing as expected? Is iCloud Photos actually a backup? explains what that sync does and doesn't protect.
  • Messages. Can you see the conversations you actually need?
  • Contacts and communication. Can you call, text, and receive messages on the new phone?
  • Important accounts. Can you sign in to banking, email, work, and the other accounts you depend on?
  • Authentication. Can you receive verification codes, and do authenticator and password-manager flows still work on the new device?
  • App-specific data. Are the apps that matter actually usable, and has their own migration or re-login step been handled?
  • Cellular. Can the new iPhone make and receive calls, and use cellular data, the way your plan expects?

Keep one distinction in mind: data being present isn't the same as your life working. A photo existing is one thing; being able to open the account that protects it is another. A banking app installed is one thing; being able to sign in is another. An authenticator app installed is one thing; generating the code it's supposed to produce is another.

After setup: use the new phone normally

Instead of waiting for everything to finish downloading, use the new phone the way you normally would. Make a call. Send a message. Open Photos. Sign in to the accounts you use. Test authentication. Check that cellular works. The goal isn't to verify every app — it's to discover whether the old phone still has a job. When you realize you've stopped reaching for it, you've found your answer.

You probably don't need to

Audit every app before using the new phone normally. Erase the old phone on the same day you open the new one — it can stay as a safety net until the new phone has proven itself. Delete or reorganize the old phone before migrating — migration and decluttering are different jobs. Panic when some apps are still downloading after setup — that's background work, not a failed move. Or keep both phones forever once the old one has no remaining job.

A simple way to decide

The new phone has everything I depend on → the old phone can move toward erasing.

One important thing is missing → identify the exact dependency before erasing the old phone.

The data is there, but an account or app isn't working → treat it as an account or app migration issue, not necessarily a failed transfer.

I don't know whether the latest photos or messages synced → verify the sync or status before letting the old phone go.

Erasing the old iPhone

Only after the new phone has proven it can replace the old one, sign out where appropriate and then erase: Settings > [your name] > Sign Out, then Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone > Erase All Content and Settings. When the erase completes properly, it removes your personal content and, on a device that used Find My, turns off Find My and Activation Lock so the next owner can set it up. If you're selling or giving the phone away, let that erase flow finish — manually deleting photos or apps isn't the same thing, because it leaves your account and activation state behind. There's no fixed waiting period in front of this step; the condition is the replacement test, not a number of days.

You don't need proof that every byte moved. You need confidence that your life moved.

The safest moment to erase the old phone isn't when Apple says setup is done. It's when you realize you no longer need the old phone to keep living normally.
