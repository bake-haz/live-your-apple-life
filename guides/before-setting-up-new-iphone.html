What Should I Do Before Setting Up My New iPhone?

For the few minutes before you open the box — the part that prevents the avoidable headaches later.

You're probably here because a new iPhone just arrived — or it's on the way — and part of you wants to open the box, sign in, and finally clear off the old phone that's been nagging you about storage. That instinct is normal. This page is about the short pause before that, which is where avoidable problems get prevented.
Don't wipe, sell, or trade in the old iPhone yet. The safe moment to let go isn't a fixed amount of time — it's when the new phone has taken over the things you actually depend on. First make sure your Apple Account, password, a recent backup, and enough iCloud space are in place. Then move your data, and only erase the old phone once you've confirmed the things you need are present and working on the new one. A few minutes of checking now is far easier than recovering something once it's gone.

Before you start

None of these are complicated. They're just worth confirming while the old phone is still in your hand:

  • The old phone still works and has some charge. You'll likely need it nearby during setup, or to make a backup first.
  • Your Apple Account and password. That's the email or phone number plus password you use for iCloud, the App Store, and Find My. You'll sign in on the new phone — if you've forgotten it, sort that out now, not halfway through setup.
  • Two-factor / a trusted device or number. Signing in to a new phone often asks you to approve from another device or enter a code. Have that handy.
  • Wi-Fi. Setup and transfers happen over Wi-Fi; a steady network avoids mid-transfer restarts.
  • A little power (or a cable). Keep both phones charged during a transfer so neither dies halfway.
  • A recent backup. iCloud Backup turned on, or a fresh computer backup — your safety net if anything goes sideways.
  • iCloud storage room. If you plan to use iCloud to move data, check you have enough space (the free tier is 5 GB, and photos and backups add up quickly).
  • The old iPhone is on Apple's currently recommended iOS version. You don't need a specific "magic" version to move, but confirming it's up to date before you start the transfer is reasonable preparation.

Decide how you want to move

There's no single method that fits everyone — it depends on what you have in hand and how much you trust your current backup. Apple's own setup steps cover the exact on-screen taps; here's the thinking behind each choice.

Quick Start (old phone to new phone)

Place the two iPhones near each other and the new one offers to copy from the old one. Both need to be on Wi-Fi with Bluetooth on, with the old phone unlocked and close by, and it helps to keep both connected to power until the transfer finishes. Your settings, accounts, and apps move over, and your photos, messages, and files follow from the transfer or from iCloud. How long it takes depends on how much data you have. You can also set the new phone up from an iCloud Backup instead, which lets the new phone get started while the rest arrives in the background.

+ Transfers your setup directly from one phone to the other, with little to do by hand.

– Needs the old phone present and working; very large libraries can take a while.

iCloud Backup / iCloud data

Set up the new phone, sign in with your Apple Account, and restore from an iCloud Backup — or let Photos and Messages sync in from iCloud. Good when the old phone isn't available, or you're moving across a distance.

+ No computer needed; works even if the old phone is already gone.

– Depends on having a current backup and enough iCloud storage; the first sync can take time on a slower network.

Computer backup (Mac or PC)

Back up the old phone with Finder on a Mac or the Apple Devices app on Windows; choose "Encrypt" to also include passwords and Health data. Then restore that backup onto the new phone.

+ Local and complete, and not dependent on iCloud storage.

– You need the computer and a cable, and it's easy to forget to do it.

The things worth checking before you start

Setup moves what's on the phone or in your account. A quick mental checklist helps you notice anything that might not follow:

  • Photos. If iCloud Photos is on, your library is already in iCloud and arrives on the new phone through that sync. If iCloud Photos is off, your photos are part of the device's data and come along with a backup or a direct transfer — they don't need to be uploaded to iCloud Photos first. What matters is that they're included in whatever you're moving. Is iCloud Photos actually a backup? goes deeper on this.
  • Contacts and calendars. If you use iCloud for them, they follow along; if they're stored "On My iPhone" only, confirm they move with your backup or transfer.
  • Messages. If Messages in iCloud is off, your message history lives on the device or backup — confirm it's included in whatever you're moving.
  • Important apps and their data. Banking, notes, chat apps with local history, fitness — some keep their own data and may need a re-login or their own backup.
  • Accounts and authentication. Authenticator apps, password managers, and email accounts often need re-verification after a move, so have recovery details ready.
  • Cellular / eSIM. Know whether your plan is eSIM (it can transfer during setup or through your carrier) or a physical SIM (you'll move the card). Don't cancel the old line until the new one is active.

After the transfer

  • Keep the old iPhone for now. Don't erase or trade it yet — it's your fallback if something didn't come across.
  • Open the things that matter. On the new phone, check Photos, Contacts, Messages, Mail, and a couple of key apps to confirm your stuff is there.
  • Confirm it actually works. Make sure calls, texts, and iMessage activate, and that you can receive a verification code if an app asks for one.
  • Let background downloads finish. Some things — like a full photo library — keep arriving even after setup looks done. Keep the old phone until you've confirmed the things you depend on are present and working, rather than waiting a fixed amount of time.

The real test: can the new iPhone replace the old one?

You don't need to check every menu. Ask one question:

If the old phone stopped working today, would I still have to rely on it for something?

  • If the answer is yes — a photo, a message thread, an account, or a code still lives only on the old phone — don't erase it yet. Keep it as your safety net until that's resolved.
  • If the answer is no — the things you depend on are already on the new phone — then you can move to signing out of and erasing the old one.

This is a better guide than a fixed waiting period: the phone you can live without is the phone you can let go of.

When is it safe to let go of the old iPhone?

You can let go once the things you depend on are confirmed on the new phone: (1) your photos, contacts, and messages are there; (2) you can sign in to your important accounts and receive codes; (3) you've checked that anything important requiring a separate sign-in — like an authenticator or a notes app — is present and working. Only then sign out of the old phone (Settings › your name › Sign Out) and erase it (Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Erase All Content and Settings). If you're selling or giving it away, erasing also removes your personal data and turns off Find My and Activation Lock — which is tied to both your Apple Account and the device — so the next person can set it up.

You probably don't need to

  • Reinstall every app by hand. Once you sign in, your apps download from the App Store — they follow your Apple Account.
  • Sort or delete a pile of photos on the old phone first. Migration and decluttering are two different jobs; do the move first, decide what to delete later.
  • Assume the move failed because the new phone is still downloading. A full photo library and some apps keep arriving in the background after setup looks finished.
  • Erase the old phone just because something hasn't appeared yet. Some items take longer to show up; confirm the things you depend on are present before letting go.
  • Buy extra software or accessories to move your data safely. A normal iPhone-to-iPhone move doesn't call for them.

You don't need to audit the entire old phone in one sitting. Confirm that the things that truly matter — photos, messages, contacts, key accounts, and your ability to communicate — have taken over on the new phone, and the rest can wait.

What makes sense for different people

  • A straightforward upgrade: use Quick Start, confirm the basics arrived, then erase the old phone once you've confirmed the things you depend on are on the new one.
  • You have a lot of data: make an encrypted computer backup as a belt-and-suspenders safety net alongside iCloud, and start the transfer with time to spare.
  • If photos are your priority: confirm iCloud Photos is on and fully synced before erasing; if your photos are device-only, back them up first.
  • You want to re-organize while switching: migration and decluttering are two different jobs. Move your data first and get the new phone working, then decide what to delete later — don't try to declutter and migrate at the same time.
