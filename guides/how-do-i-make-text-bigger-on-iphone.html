How do I make text bigger and easier to read on my iPhone?

You don't have to squint. One setting makes nearly all text larger, everywhere.

You're probably here because the text on your iPhone is small enough that you're holding it closer, turning the brightness all the way up, or avoiding reading things on it. Maybe your eyes have changed, or maybe the phone just came set smaller than you like. Either way, it feels like the phone wasn't made for you.
Direct answer: open Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Larger Text, turn on Larger Accessibility Sizes, then drag the slider right until the sample text feels comfortable. That changes the text size across most of the phone at once — not one app at a time. For an extra boost, also turn on Bold Text on that same screen. Nothing about this is permanent; you can slide it back down whenever you want.

The setting that does the work

This is the one to remember, because it scales text system-wide:

  • Open Settings.
  • Tap Accessibility. (It's in the main list, not hidden behind Display & Brightness.)
  • Tap Display & Text Size.
  • Tap Larger Text.
  • Turn on Larger Accessibility Sizes. This unlocks the biggest sizes, which is often what makes the difference.
  • Drag the slider at the bottom right until the sample sentence is easy to read.

The change applies as you drag, so you can feel it before you leave the screen. Messages, Settings, Mail, Notes, and most other built-in apps all pick up the new size.

Two extras that help a lot

On that same Display & Text Size screen, a couple of switches make text easier on the eyes:

  • Bold Text. Makes every letter thicker, which many people find noticeably easier to read — especially at smaller sizes. Turning it on restarts the phone once to apply, which is normal.
  • Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency. If text sits on a blurry or colorful background and is hard to pick out, these make backgrounds more solid so text stands out.

You don't need all of them. Most people get what they want from Larger Text plus Bold Text, and leave the rest off.

If size alone isn't enough, make buttons clearer

Some people read the words fine but still miss what's actually tappable. Two more switches on that same Display & Text Size screen help:

  • Button Shapes. Turns many text buttons into underlined, button-shaped links so it's obvious what you can press. Handy in apps where a plain word like "Done" or "Learn More" doesn't look clickable.
  • On/Off Labels. Adds a visible + and − to toggle switches, so you don't have to judge whether a slim switch is left or right. Useful if the standard green/white toggle is subtle for you.

These don't change text size, but they reduce the "where do I tap?" friction that often comes with small interfaces. Turn on whichever one helps; leaving both off is also normal.

You probably don't need to

  • Change the text size inside every app. Once you set it in Accessibility, most apps follow along. A few (like some reading apps) have their own size control, but you rarely need to touch those.
  • Buy anything. This is a built-in setting. No app, no subscription, no accessory required.
  • Worry that you "broke" the look of the phone. Larger text is a normal preference, not a problem to fix. Plenty of people run their phones at the largest size.
  • Max it out by default. The very largest setting can push buttons off-screen in a few old apps. Stop where it's comfortable, not where the slider ends.

What if you want everything bigger, not just text?

Larger Text enlarges words. If you'd also like icons, buttons, and the whole layout to grow, there's a separate control:

  • Settings > Display & Brightness > View (labeled "Display Zoom" on some versions).
  • Choose Zoomed instead of Standard, then Set.
  • The phone restarts once and everything is physically larger.

This is stronger medicine than Larger Text — it changes the whole interface, so it's best when small text is only part of the problem and you want a bigger phone experience overall. Try Larger Text first; reach for Zoomed View only if that isn't enough.

If some text still looks small

A few spots don't follow the system text size, and that's expected rather than a setting you missed:

  • Photos and videos you receive are whatever size they were sent — no setting changes those.
  • Some websites force their own small text; pinch to zoom, or use the page's own reader view if offered.
  • An app that ignores the setting is the app's choice, not a phone fault. If one app bothers you, check whether it has its own text-size option inside its own settings.

None of that means the phone is stuck small. The system setting covers the parts you read most.

One thing worth knowing: text size is a device-wide setting, not per person. If you share the phone with someone who prefers smaller text, you can't keep two sizes at once — you'd either meet in the middle or leave Display Zoom off and let them zoom where they need it.

A simple way to decide

Set the slider one step bigger than "fine" — to "comfortable." If you have to lean in to confirm it's readable, it's still too small; if you can glance and understand, it's right. You can always nudge it later, so there's no wrong answer today.
