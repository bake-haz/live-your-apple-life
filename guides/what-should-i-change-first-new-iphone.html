What Should I Change First on a New iPhone?

It works. It just doesn't feel like yours yet. That's a quick fix — not a 20-setting project.

You're probably here because the phone is set up and usable, but something feels slightly off — not broken, just not fitted to you. You've seen those "20 settings to change immediately" lists and felt a low-grade pressure to do all of them. You don't have to. This is about the handful of changes that shape daily comfort.
Short answer: change only what touches your everyday feel, and skip the rest. Notifications, Control Center, Lock Screen, and a couple of display or Focus tweaks cover most people. The defaults are sane; nothing here is required. Pick the two or three that match your day, and tune more later when something annoys you. The phone gets "yours" through small adjustments, not a big setup session.

Why the "must-change" lists miss the point

Those lists push the same twenty switches at everyone. But your life isn't everyone's. A photographer and a person who mostly calls their mom need different things from the same phone. The useful question isn't "what can I change?" but "what keeps rubbing me the wrong way?" Start there, and the list gets short.

The areas worth a look

  • Notifications. Which apps genuinely deserve to buzz you? Trim the rest. This single change does more for daily calm than almost anything else.
  • Control Center. Swipe down from the top-right to open it. Add what you actually use (Wallet, flashlight, Low Power Mode) and drop what you don't. It's your quick panel — make it yours.
  • Lock Screen. You can add widgets (weather, calendar, a photo) and change what a swipe shows. A calendar or photos widget up front saves taps.
  • Home Screen. Arrange apps you reach for, tuck the rest into the App Library, and add a widget or two. Fewer pages often feels calmer than many.
  • Focus. Work, Sleep, and Personal modes silence the right notifications at the right time. Even one Focus (Do Not Disturb while sleeping) is a real quality-of-life win.
  • Keyboard. Text replacement (type "addr" → your address) and autocorrect tweaks save repetition. Small, daily, cumulative.
  • Display and text size. If anything is hard to read, bump text size or bold text in Display & Brightness. Comfort beats default every time.
  • Battery. Turn on Low Power Mode when you're low; check Battery Health so you know the battery's condition. Don't obsess over percentages — manage, don't monitor.
  • Privacy permissions. Review which apps have your location or photos. Tightening a few is good hygiene, not a chore.
  • Default apps. Since iOS 18.2 you can set a different default browser, email, and more in Settings > Apps > Default Apps — if Apple's defaults bug you daily.
  • Widgets, Photos, sounds. A Photos widget on the Home Screen, or a gentler notification sound, are the kind of touch that makes it feel personal.

Where to find each setting

You don't have to hunt. The main ones live in a few predictable places:

  • Notifications: Settings > Notifications.
  • Control Center: open it, long-press any control to customize, or go to Settings > Control Center.
  • Lock Screen widgets: long-press an empty area of the Lock Screen, then tap Customize.
  • Focus: Settings > Focus.
  • Display and text: Settings > Display & Brightness, then Text Size or Bold Text.
  • Default apps: Settings > Apps > Default Apps.
  • Battery: Settings > Battery.

Pick by how you live

Want it quieter: prune notifications first, then set a Sleep Focus. A good first move: open Settings > Notifications and turn off alerts for the three apps that buzz you most.

Works a lot: a Work Focus, a calendar widget on the Lock Screen, and mail where you want it. Start with one Work Focus that silences personal noise during the day.

Lives in photos: a Photos widget, make sure iCloud Photos is on, and glance at your iCloud storage so it never surprises you. First step: confirm iCloud Photos is on and the library is syncing.

Likes it minimal: fewer Home Screen pages, lean on the App Library, one clean widget. Try hiding every Home Screen page but one and living from the App Library for a week.

Often out and about: Wallet and Find My in Control Center, Low Power Mode handy, offline maps downloaded. Add Wallet and Find My to Control Center today — both pay off the first time you need them.

Just switched from Android: set your default apps if it matters, learn the gestures, and give it a week before changing more. Concrete start: set your default browser and email in Settings > Apps > Default Apps if Apple's defaults bug you.

On battery anxiety

Battery worry is its own category of "doesn't feel right." Two calm facts: iOS can pause charging at around 80% to reduce long-term wear (Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging > Charge Limit), which is a quiet "work better" change if you keep the phone plugged in overnight. And you don't need to watch the percentage all day — Low Power Mode is there when you actually need it. Deciding how much battery attention you want is itself one of the first changes worth making.

What you probably don't need to worry about

The factory settings are fine. You won't break anything by leaving most of them alone, and every change here can be undone in seconds. There's no setting you're "supposed" to flip. The phone becomes yours through a few choices that match your day — not by matching someone else's list.

Start with one small thing

If the whole list feels like a chore, do a single change today — turn on one Focus, or prune notifications for the three noisiest apps. Live with that for a day. The phone starts to feel like yours through a few small choices, not a big setup session, and you can always come back for the next one when something actually bothers you.
