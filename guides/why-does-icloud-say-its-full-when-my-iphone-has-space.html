Why Does iCloud Say It's Full When My iPhone Has Space?

"iCloud storage" and "iPhone storage" are two different spaces. Clearing one doesn't help the other.

You're probably here because you saw an "iCloud Storage Full" notice, but when you check the phone it shows plenty of free space. It feels contradictory — and it makes the alert seem wrong. It isn't. They're measuring two unrelated things.
Direct answer: iCloud storage is the online space tied to your Apple Account — it holds your iCloud Backup, iCloud Photos, iCloud Drive files, and a few synced items. iPhone storage is the space inside the phone itself, for apps, photos, and system files. Apple's free iCloud allowance is small (commonly 5 GB), so it fills up long before your phone does. Freeing space on the phone does nothing for iCloud, and vice versa — they're separate meters.

Two separate "full" meters

  • iPhone storage — the capacity built into the device. Covered in Why is my iPhone storage full when I don't have that many apps?
  • iCloud storage — your account's cloud space, shared across every device signed in with the same Apple Account.

That second point matters: your iCloud space is one pool used by all your devices and by several features at once. One phone with lots of free space says nothing about whether that shared pool is full.

What actually counts against iCloud storage

The usual occupants of that shared pool:

  • iCloud Backup — the automatic, whole-device backup of your iPhone (and any other Apple devices on the account).
  • iCloud Photos — your photo and video library, if you've turned on Photos syncing to iCloud. This is usually the biggest single user, because videos are large.
  • iCloud Drive — files you've saved there, including some app data.
  • iCloud Mail — only if you use an iCloud email address with a large mailbox.
  • Messages in iCloud — if enabled, your message history syncs to iCloud too.

Why it fills up

With only a small free allowance, a normal life fills it: years of photos and videos in iCloud Photos, plus a backup of the phone (and often an old backup of a phone you no longer use, still sitting there). Most "full" notices come down to photos plus stale backups — not because anything is broken.

How to see what's using it

Open Settings, tap your name at the top, then iCloud > Manage Storage (also labeled "iCloud Storage"). You'll see a list of everything in the pool with its size. That list tells you exactly what to address — no guessing.

How to free it without losing what matters

  • Delete backups you no longer need. An old phone you've erased or sold may still have a backup in the list. Removing it is safe and often frees the most space.
  • Stop backing up apps you don't care about. In Manage Storage, you can turn off iCloud Backup for specific apps so they're excluded from the next backup.
  • Empty Recently Deleted in Photos. Photos you delete from iCloud Photos linger for about 30 days and still count against storage until they're permanently removed.
  • For photos specifically, export before you trim. If iCloud Photos is the big user, the safe way to shrink it is to first save your originals to a Mac, PC, or another backup, then remove them from iCloud — not to delete them blindly (deleting from iCloud Photos removes them from every device).

Do you need to pay for more?

Upgrading to iCloud+ (Apple's paid storage) is optional. You only need it if you want Apple to keep more in the cloud. If you'd rather not, backing up to a Mac or PC gives you a complete offline copy instead. Either is fine — the alert is a nudge, not an emergency.

You probably don't need to

  • Delete photos from iCloud to "just free space." That removes them from all your devices. Export them somewhere safe first.
  • Confuse the two meters. Clearing phone storage won't silence the iCloud notice, and clearing iCloud won't free the phone.
  • Rush to buy storage if a stale old-device backup is all that's needed.

When to actually pay attention

The one real consequence of a full iCloud is that iCloud Backup stops completing — your phone isn't being backed up to the cloud until there's room. That's worth fixing, because a phone that isn't backed up can mean lost data if it's lost or broken. You fix it by freeing space in the pool or by backing up to a computer instead. See Do you need iCloud Backup? and Is iCloud Photos actually a backup? for the bigger picture.

What to do next

  • Open Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Manage Storage and check what's using the space.
  • If photos are the issue, read Is iCloud Photos actually a backup? before deleting anything.
  • If your phone itself is low, see Why is my iPhone storage full when I don't have that many apps?
