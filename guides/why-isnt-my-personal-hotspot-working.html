Why Isn't My Personal Hotspot Working?

Personal Hotspot shares your iPhone's cellular connection — so the real question is which part is failing: the feature, the cellular path, or the device trying to connect.

You're probably here because the Personal Hotspot option disappeared, your laptop can't find your iPhone, the hotspot turns on but nothing can join, or it worked yesterday and not today. Underneath is a quieter worry: is my iPhone broken? You don't have to take that on faith in either direction. A hotspot has a few distinct layers, and the state you're looking at tells you which layer failed.
Short answer: before changing any settings, find out which part is failing — the hotspot feature itself, the iPhone's cellular connection, or the device trying to connect. What you see right now points to where to look:
  • Option missing or greyed out — the cellular / carrier path.
  • Option exists, other device can't see it — discovery and device setup.
  • Visible but won't connect — password, compatibility, or connection state.
  • Connected but no internet — the cellular / carrier / internet path.

What Personal Hotspot actually shares

Personal Hotspot doesn't share the Wi-Fi your iPhone is joined to. It shares the iPhone's cellular data connection as a small network of its own. That one fact sorts a lot of confusion: the hotspot needs cellular data to exist at all, and if the cellular path is down, the hotspot has nothing to share — no matter what its switch says.

Start with what you can actually see

Match the state in front of you to one of these. Each points to a different layer:

  • "Personal Hotspot" isn't there or is greyed out. Open Settings > Cellular first: the hotspot rides on cellular data, so confirm Cellular Data is on and working. If cellular data works but the option is still missing or unavailable, the next gate is your carrier — Personal Hotspot requires a wireless provider that supports it, some plans charge extra for it, and the number of devices that can join at once depends on the carrier and iPhone model. That's a plan question, not a settings hunt.
  • It's there, but the other device can't see it. Make sure Allow Others to Join is on, and stay on the Personal Hotspot screen until the other device connects — Apple's own steps say to keep that screen open. Keep the two devices close, and check that the connecting device's Wi-Fi is on. If the other device is a Mac, iPad, or another iPhone, see Instant Hotspot below.
  • It appears, but refuses to connect. Check the hotspot's Wi-Fi password — it's shown right on the Personal Hotspot screen, and it needs at least eight characters. On the device trying to join, toggle Wi-Fi off and back on, in case it's holding an old connection state. If one particular device still won't join, Maximize Compatibility (below) exists for that kind of wireless mismatch.
  • It connects, but the other device has no internet. Discovery worked — the hotspot itself is running. The question moves to the iPhone's cellular data, the carrier plan, or the cellular network: check whether the iPhone itself can load a page right now, and whether cellular data is available at all. Connected to the hotspot is not the same as internet available.

Instant Hotspot is a shortcut, not the feature

Instant Hotspot is the quicker way Apple devices connect to each other's hotspot — it isn't a separate feature with its own switch. It shows up when the devices are signed in to the same Apple Account or belong to the same Family Sharing group, are near each other, and have Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on. If Instant Hotspot doesn't appear on your Mac or iPad, that doesn't mean Personal Hotspot is broken: the regular path — choosing the iPhone's network from the Wi-Fi list and entering its password — still works.

Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, USB — use them as diagnostics

The hotspot supports three connection methods: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and USB. Treat them as diagnostic routes rather than tricks. If Wi-Fi discovery fails but a USB cable connects, you've learned something useful: the iPhone can still provide its connection, and the problem is specific to the wireless path — discovery or authentication. If USB also fails to give the other device internet, the evidence points back toward the hotspot or the cellular connection itself.

Maximize Compatibility is a compatibility tool

When a specific device struggles to join or stay joined over the wireless connection, Maximize Compatibility changes how the hotspot broadcasts so that kind of device can connect. Apple notes that with it on, internet performance and Wi-Fi security may be reduced — so it's a tool for a known mismatch, not a default. Turn it on for the device that won't cooperate; leave it off otherwise.

What it costs

Personal Hotspot uses your iPhone's cellular data and power: whatever the connected device does counts against your plan, some carriers charge separately for hotspot use, and the iPhone works harder while it's broadcasting. Apple also notes that a computer your iPhone is plugged into for charging may connect to the hotspot on its own. None of this is a malfunction — it's the cost of sharing a connection. Turning off Allow Others to Join when you're done keeps both in check.

If it worked yesterday and not today

Walk the same states instead of hunting for what changed: Is the option still there, and can it turn on? Can the other device see it? Does it connect — and does that connection actually reach the internet? Whichever state changed is the layer that changed with it: the cellular plan or data, a switch on the phone, or the device trying to connect. When nothing obvious has changed, Apple's own order stays short: confirm the hotspot is on and your plan supports it, restart both devices, make sure both are running the latest software — and only then consider heavier steps. Nothing here requires a reset by default.

You probably don't need to

You probably don't need to:

  • reset the whole iPhone because the hotspot failed once;
  • treat your normal Wi-Fi settings as hotspot settings — the hotspot shares cellular data, not your router;
  • start changing Bluetooth or network settings before you know which layer failed;
  • assume the iPhone's hardware is broken because a hotspot disappeared;
  • reconfigure the entire hotspot because one device connected but has no internet.

Identify the layer first — the fix lives in the layer that failed.

A simple way to decide

  • Hotspot option is missing — check cellular service and carrier support.
  • Hotspot is available but not visible — check Allow Others to Join, the connecting device's Wi-Fi, and the Instant Hotspot conditions.
  • Hotspot is visible but won't connect — check the password, compatibility, or retry with another connection method.
  • Hotspot connects but internet doesn't work — investigate the cellular service, the carrier, and data availability.

You don't need to troubleshoot everything at once. You only need to identify which part failed first.
