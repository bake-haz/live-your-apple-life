Why Isn't My Personal Hotspot Working?

It's a feature with three requirements — miss one and it quietly won't appear.

You're probably here because you turned on what you thought was a hotspot, or looked for it on another device, and nothing showed up. Personal Hotspot is one of those features that depends on several things lining up at once — carrier support, cellular data, and a specific switch — so when one is missing it fails silently instead of telling you why.
Direct answer: Personal Hotspot needs cellular data, a carrier plan that includes it, and "Allow Others to Join" turned on. It is not the same as your normal Wi-Fi, and turning on Wi-Fi on the iPhone does not create a hotspot. Once those three are true, the other device connects through Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or USB — and nearby Apple devices can use the quicker Instant Hotspot.

Where the hotspot actually lives

Open Settings > Personal Hotspot (on some versions it's under Settings > Cellular > Personal Hotspot). The one switch that matters is Allow Others to Join. Turn it on. If the whole Personal Hotspot option is dimmed or missing, that usually points to the carrier or cellular data, not a setting you can toggle. If it's truly unavailable, go to Settings > Cellular and make sure Cellular Data is on — Personal Hotspot rides on top of it.

Why it might not appear on the other device

  • Your carrier may not include it. Personal Hotspot requires a wireless carrier that supports it, and some plans charge extra or don't offer it. If the option is missing entirely, this is the most likely reason — not something a setting can fix.
  • Cellular data is off. No cellular data, no hotspot. The feature literally shares the phone's mobile connection.
  • The phone is already on a Wi-Fi network. This surprises people: when your iPhone is connected to Wi-Fi, Personal Hotspot typically won't broadcast, because the phone assumes you'd use that Wi-Fi instead. Disconnect from Wi-Fi and the hotspot becomes available.
  • The other device is too far or has Wi-Fi/Bluetooth off. For Instant Hotspot (Apple devices on the same account or Family Sharing), both devices need to be near each other with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on. Stay on the Personal Hotspot screen on the iPhone until the other device connects.
  • Too many devices already joined. The number of devices that can connect at once depends on your carrier and iPhone model.

Two switches worth knowing

In Personal Hotspot settings you can set a Wi-Fi Password (at least eight characters). If a device can't connect, an old or weak password is a common cause — set a fresh one. And if a connection keeps dropping or an older laptop struggles, turn on Maximize Compatibility in the same settings; it trades a little speed for broader compatibility. Apple's own guidance suggests both of these when the hotspot isn't working after updates and a restart.

Three ways the other device can connect

The hotspot isn't only "Wi-Fi." A Mac, iPad, or another iPhone near yours can use Instant Hotspot — it appears in the Wi-Fi list automatically when both are on the same Apple Account or Family Sharing group, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on. You can also connect over Bluetooth (pair the two devices first), or with a USB cable to a computer (on Windows that needs the Apple Devices or iTunes app installed, and you may need to tap "Trust" on the iPhone). If one method won't connect, try another — they fail for different reasons, so a USB cable often works when Wi-Fi discovery doesn't.

What it costs you: data and battery

A hotspot shares your mobile data, so whatever the other device does — video, updates, backups — counts against your plan. Some carriers charge separately for hotspot use; check yours. It also uses more battery on the iPhone, since it's transmitting a network. Turning off "Allow Others to Join" when you're done avoids surprise data use (on a laptop, simply plugging the iPhone in to charge can quietly reconnect the hotspot). None of this is a malfunction — it's the nature of sharing a connection.

Why it worked yesterday and not today

The common, non-scary reasons: cellular data got turned off (by you or an automation), the phone joined a Wi-Fi network so the hotspot stopped broadcasting, the carrier had a brief outage, or the other device moved out of range. None require a reset. Walk the list above — cellular on, Allow Others to Join on, Wi-Fi disconnected on the phone, devices close — and it usually returns without a call to anyone.

The hotspot name is your phone name

The network name other devices see is simply your iPhone's name. If you'd like it to read something clearer (say, "Jordan's iPhone" instead of a model number), change it in Settings > General > About > Name — the hotspot name updates to match. A clearer name just makes the right network easier to pick out in a crowded list, which saves a confused tap or two.

A simple way to decide

If the Personal Hotspot option is missing or dimmed → it's the carrier or cellular data, not a setting you can toggle. If the option is there but the other device can't find it → check that the phone isn't on Wi-Fi, that both devices are close with radios on, and try a different connection method (USB or Bluetooth). That split tells you whether to call your carrier or just adjust a switch.

You probably don't need to

Don't confuse Personal Hotspot with "sharing" your home Wi-Fi — it's the opposite: it shares your cellular data, not your router. Don't expect it to work while the phone is comfortably on Wi-Fi; that's by design. And don't assume a missing hotspot means a broken phone; in most regions the carrier is the gatekeeper.
