Why Does an App Still Seem to Work After I Closed It?

Closing an app is not the same as disabling every feature connected to it.

You're probably here because you swiped an app up from the App Switcher, then a moment later a notification arrived, audio kept playing, new content showed up, or you noticed battery or cellular activity you didn't expect. The reaction is natural: “I closed it.” Exactly — but “closed” can mean something narrower than “nothing connected to this app can happen again.” That distinction is the whole point of this page.
Direct answer: removing an app from the App Switcher ends that app's recent-app state, but it does not automatically disable every independent mechanism that may still produce notifications, downloads, media, location behavior, or other activity associated with the app. Don't assume the app itself is continuously executing in the background; whether that is true depends on the specific mechanism involved. Closing the app is a real action — just not the same as turning off the app's services.

First, identify what “still working” actually means

Pin down what you are actually seeing. The same feeling — “it's still doing something” — can come from very different causes:

  • A notification arrives. The app may deliver it through Apple's notification system. A notification is not proof the app is constantly running.
  • Audio or media continues. Check whether a media session is still active and which app or control is actually responsible — not every audio app behaves the same way.
  • New content appears when you reopen the app. That doesn't prove the app ran continuously after you closed it. The content may have refreshed when you opened it, or through a different system mechanism.
  • Location still matters. Location access has its own permission and system behavior; “closed” does not mean location stops.
  • Storage or downloads changed. Identify the actual download or sync mechanism before blaming the App Switcher gesture.
  • Battery or cellular usage changed. Use Settings > Battery and Settings > Cellular as evidence — don't guess which app caused it.

This is the diagnostic heart of the article: name the symptom before naming the cause.

What closing an app actually does

Removing an app from the App Switcher removes it from the recent-app view and ends its foreground session. It is not a universal “disable everything” command. Other Apple mechanisms — notifications, sync, location, media — are controlled separately, and closing one app does not reach them.

Closing is still right when an app is frozen, when you want to end its session now, or as a troubleshooting first step. The gesture matters; it just isn't a master switch.

Background App Refresh is one mechanism, not the explanation

Background App Refresh lets suspended apps check for new content under system-controlled conditions. You manage it in Settings > General > Background App Refresh, where you can turn it off or limit it to Wi-Fi or Wi-Fi & Cellular Data. When you close an app from the App Switcher, it may not be able to run or check for new content until you open it again.

But Background App Refresh is a single mechanism. It does not explain every notification, every media session, every sync, or every location event. Treat it as one suspect, not the verdict.

Notifications are a separate system

If you close an app and later receive a notification, that does not mean the app reopened itself. Notification delivery is handled by the system, not by the app sitting open on your screen. Receiving an alert is not proof the app is actively executing. For the fuller picture, see Why do iPhone notifications feel so different from Android?.

Location is a separate permission model

An app's location access is controlled separately from the App Switcher. Whether location activity can continue depends on the app's location permission and the system behavior associated with that permission — not on whether you swiped the app away. You adjust it in Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services.

Media and audio are separate too

If media keeps playing after you swipe an app away, first identify which app is the current playback source and whether the system media controls still show an active session. Another connected device may be controlling playback. This is not the same as “the app is still open,” and it isn't a reason to treat every audio app the same way. If a Bluetooth device is involved, see Why won't my Bluetooth device connect to my iPhone?.

Cloud sync, files, and photos

A photo, file, or cloud-linked change can happen because a sync system is moving data between your devices and a service — not because the app runs in the background. If iCloud Photos is the part you noticed, that's a sync library, not a background process: see Is iCloud Photos actually a backup?.

Closing the app is not the same as removing its permissions

This is the mental model worth keeping:

  • Closing ≠ notifications disabled. The app can still alert you.
  • Closing ≠ location permissions revoked. Its location access stays as you set it.
  • Closing ≠ account signed out. Your login is untouched.
  • Closing ≠ cloud synchronization disabled. Sync keeps working by design.
  • Closing ≠ subscription cancelled. Payments and access continue.
  • Closing ≠ media session necessarily ended. Audio may keep playing until you pause it.

One gesture should not be read as changing five unrelated systems. Each of those is its own control.

When the behavior deserves a closer look

Focus on evidence, not anxiety:

  • Battery usage changed unexpectedly — check Settings > Battery to see which app is responsible.
  • Cellular usage changed unexpectedly — check Settings > Cellular for the app or service using it.
  • A permission prompt appears unexpectedly — treat it as a permission or account event, not ordinary background activity.
  • The app repeatedly downloads content you didn't expect — inspect its own settings and the relevant system download mechanism.
  • The app behaves differently every time you reopen it — work out whether you're seeing a sync state, a setting, or actual app behavior.
  • Nothing measurable happened; the app simply reappears in the App Switcher — that alone is not evidence of background activity.

Before you blame the phone — or yourself

Seeing something happen after you closed an app doesn't prove you did the gesture wrong. It also doesn't prove the app is ignoring you. The useful response is to identify the behavior before changing the phone. You don't have to understand every background process — you only need to name the one you noticed.

You probably don't need to

  • Force-close every app repeatedly. Closing is for a frozen or misbehaving app, not a daily reset.
  • Disable Background App Refresh for every app before identifying the symptom. Turn it off only for the app whose background refresh is actually a problem.
  • Turn off every notification. Adjust the one app that bugs you in Settings > Notifications.
  • Revoke every location permission because one app behaved unexpectedly. Change the specific app's setting instead.
  • Reset the iPhone because one app appeared to keep doing something. A single app rarely calls for a full reset.
  • Assume a notification means the app was continuously executing in the background. Notifications arrive through the system, not an open app.
  • Assume closing an app disables every cloud, account, or media mechanism connected to it. Those are separate controls.

Don't use a bigger system-wide change to solve a behavior you haven't identified yet.

A simple way to decide

  • I closed it and still got a notification. Treat it as a notification-system question, not proof the app is running.
  • I closed it and audio kept playing. Identify the active media session and its source.
  • I closed it and the app still seems to affect location. Inspect its current location permission and the behavior involved.
  • I closed it but data or storage changed. Check Battery and Cellular, then identify the actual mechanism.
  • I only see the app again when I reopen it. That alone does not establish background activity.
  • I still cannot explain what happened. Isolate the exact symptom before changing system settings.
